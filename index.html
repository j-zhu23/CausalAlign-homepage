Abstract

Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components.

First, we diagnose how precise ego-motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we construct a delta-token space from a 3D foundation model and align VGM representations at causally identified positions. Delta tokens encode changes between consecutive frames, allowing the alignment to focus on ego-motion rather than static scene content.

With Wan2.2 fine-tuned on DL3DV and RealEstate10K, CausalAlign preserves visual quality while improving motion accuracy, geometric consistency, and temporal smoothness. For example, it reduces geometric consistency errors by more than 20% relative to the base model. Moreover, using the fine-tuned VGM, CausalAlign improves downstream world action models (WAMs) on the RoboCasa365 benchmark. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields gains in downstream embodied models on motion-intensive tasks.

Method

CausalAlign identifies where a video generation model begins to forget ego-motion, then aligns those internal states with motion-centric delta tokens from a 3D foundation model.

01

Build the teacher delta-token space

Encode changes between frames with a 3D foundation model, separating ego-motion from static scene content.

02

Train the VGM projector

Map VGM feature transitions into the teacher delta-token space, then freeze the projector.

03

Fine-tune selected VGM layers

Apply delta-token supervision through LoRA adapters at the layers identified by causal diagnosis.
